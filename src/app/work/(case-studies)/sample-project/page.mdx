## Overview

This is a sample case study. Replace it with your own story. A useful structure
for product design case studies:

1. **Context** — what's the product, who's the team, what triggered this work.
2. **Problem** — what was broken, and how you knew (data, research, support tickets).
3. **Approach** — how you framed it, what you explored, what you cut.
4. **Solution** — the design, with annotated screens and key decisions.
5. **Impact** — what changed after launch, and what you'd do differently.

## Context

The company offered a consumer banking app. New users had to complete a
seven-step setup flow before they could move any money, and roughly a third of
them dropped off before finishing.

## Problem

Through funnel analysis and five moderated usability sessions, three issues
surfaced:

- Identity verification came too early, before users understood the value.
- Error messages were written for compliance, not for people.
- Nothing reassured users their money and data were safe.

> "I got to the ID photo screen and just closed the app. I wasn't ready to hand
> that over yet." — Participant 3

## Approach

I reframed onboarding as *earning the right to ask for more*. Each step should
deliver something before requesting sensitive information.

![Placeholder for a flow diagram](/projects/sample-project/flow.png)

## Solution

The redesigned flow front-loads a personalized preview and defers verification
until the user initiates their first transfer. Trust signals (FDIC insurance,
encryption, a real support contact) appear contextually rather than in a wall of
fine print.

## Impact

- Time-to-first-transaction dropped **40%**.
- Onboarding completion rose from **68% to 84%**.
- Support tickets tagged *"verification"* fell by a third.

If I did it again, I'd invest earlier in a shared component for form validation —
we rebuilt it three times across surfaces.
